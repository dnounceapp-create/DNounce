You found a contractor. They have great photos, a friendly pitch, and a price that fits your budget. But how do you actually know they're legitimate before you hand over thousands of dollars?

Contractor fraud and poor workmanship are among the most common consumer complaints in the United States. The good news: most of the warning signs are visible before you sign anything — if you know where to look.

## Step 1: Verify Their License

Every state has a contractor licensing board. Before anything else, look up your contractor's license number in your state's official database.

What to check:
- Is the license active and current?
- Does the license cover the type of work you need (general contractor vs. electrician vs. plumber)?
- Are there any disciplinary actions or complaints on file?

In New York, you can check through the Department of State. In California, use the CSLB (Contractors State License Board). Most states have a free online lookup.

If a contractor can't provide a license number, that's a hard stop.

## Step 2: Check Their Insurance

A licensed contractor should carry two types of insurance:

**General liability insurance** — covers damage to your property during the job.
**Workers' compensation** — covers their workers if someone gets hurt on your property.

Ask for certificates of insurance and call the insurance company to verify the policy is active. Don't just accept a document — verify it.

## Step 3: Search Their Reputation

Licenses and insurance tell you they're legal. Reputation tells you whether they're trustworthy.

Where to look:
- **BBB (Better Business Bureau)** — search for complaints and resolution history
- **Google reviews** — look at the negative reviews specifically; how did they respond?
- **DNounce** — a platform where both sides of a dispute are heard; search for their name to see if any unresolved disputes exist
- **Local Facebook groups** — neighborhood groups often surface contractors that other platforms miss

## Step 4: Ask for References — Then Actually Call Them

Every reputable contractor will give you references. Most people never call them.

When you call, ask:
- Did the project finish on time and on budget?
- Were there any surprises, and how were they handled?
- Would you hire them again?

The last question is the most important. A hesitation tells you more than a yes.

## Step 5: Get Everything in Writing

A legitimate contractor will have no problem putting the scope of work, timeline, payment schedule, and warranty in writing. If they resist a written contract, walk away.

Your contract should include:
- Itemized breakdown of work and materials
- Payment schedule tied to milestones (not time)
- Start and estimated completion date
- What happens if the project goes over budget
- Warranty on labor and materials

## Red Flags to Watch For

- Demands full payment upfront
- No physical address or PO box only
- Significantly lower bid than all competitors
- Pressure to decide immediately
- Can't produce a license or insurance certificate
- No written contract offered

## The Bottom Line

Hiring a contractor is one of the largest financial decisions most people make outside of buying a home. The 30 minutes you spend verifying credentials upfront can save you months of disputes and thousands of dollars.
